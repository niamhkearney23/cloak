# Cloak: pricing options and pilot plan

> **Every price on this page is a suggestion for the owner to decide.** None has been agreed, tested with buyers or offered to anyone. Prices are in ringgit (RM), per year, and exclude any service tax. The owner should confirm with a tax adviser whether service tax (SST) applies and at what rate.

## 1. Decisions for the owner before any price is quoted

1. **Pilots on real matters need a licence key only you can make.** The double checker is built, but today every copy of Cloak is an evaluation copy: it exports cloaked copies only from matters ticked as "made-up test documents only". A pilot on real matters needs a Cloak licence issued by you, which needs you to make the vendor licence key, build Cloak with its public half and issue the firm a licence (see the licence guide). Until then a pilot can only use made-up or sample documents. Option A: start pilots now as made-up "dry runs". Option B: make the licence key and a licensed build first, then run pilots that can move to real matters in week 3.
2. **Which pricing model** (section 2): per seat, firm licence, or both.
3. **Free or paid pilot** (section 3).
4. **Founding customer terms** for the first firms.
5. **Support** you can promise (hours, response time, who answers).
6. **Licence terms**: what evaluation mode allows (the offline licence is built: with no licence, or one that has ended, Cloak exports only matters ticked as made-up test documents; reading, review, the chronology and restoring drafts always work; seats are shown but not counted; no licence can be issued until you make the vendor licence key and build Cloak with it), what happens when a licence lapses (already so: Cloak still opens existing matters and restores drafts, so a firm is never locked out of its own keys), refunds and termination.
7. **Code signing**: budget for Windows and Apple code signing certificates before selling widely, so firms do not see "publisher unknown" (the installers are ready to be signed once certificates are bought; the first signed build has not been tried yet).
8. **Contract and terms of use**: have a Malaysian advocate draft the licence agreement. It should state that Cloak lowers the risk and does not remove it, and that Lawgistics receives no client data.

Includes: the installers, all updates released during the year, email support [hours and response time to be decided: see decision 5], and one onboarding session.

Good for: small firms and firms that want a few disputes lawyers to start.

### Option C: pilot pricing and early discounts

- **30-day pilot:** free for up to 5 users, or a fee of RM 1,500 that is fully credited against the first year if the firm buys within 30 days of the pilot ending. A small fee tends to get more attention from the firm; a free pilot is easier to agree.
- **Founding customer discount:** 30% off the first year for the first five firms that sign, with the year 1 price held for year 2, in return for structured feedback and (only with the firm's written permission) being named as a customer.
- **Multi-year:** 10% off for a two-year commitment paid yearly.

### What it is reasonable to say about value

Say only what can be measured. Cloak's value to a firm is in (1) time saved preparing documents for an AI tool compared with redacting by hand, (2) consistency across a whole folder (one tag per person in every file, across the other spellings and short forms it recognises, including parts people miss, such as tracked changes, comments and file names), (3) putting real details back into the AI's draft on the firm's own computer, and (4) a record of who reviewed and exported what. The pilot below is designed to measure (1) and (2) in each firm. Do not quote savings until a firm has measured them.

## 3. The 30-day pilot plan

### Purpose

To let a firm see, on documents it trusts, what Cloak catches, what it misses, how long it takes, and whether its lawyers would use it, and to decide at day 30 whether to buy, extend or stop.

### Rules for every pilot

- **Lawgistics never receives the firm's documents.** Cloak runs on the firm's computers. Support uses made-up matters only. If something fails, Cloak can save a support file that the firm checks and sends itself; it holds staff names and logins and counts, but no document text or client details.
- **Made-up documents first.** Weeks 1 and 2 use the Cloak sample matter (Try a sample matter on the home screen) and a made-up folder the firm writes itself. Real matters are used only in week 3, only if the firm has a Cloak licence from Lawgistics, and only if the firm's sponsoring partner and risk contact agree in writing. Without a licence, every copy is an evaluation copy and export only works on matters ticked as made-up test documents. No licence can be issued until the owner makes the vendor licence key.
- **Misses are reported** in the pilot report, including ones nobody asked about.
- **The firm's test folder stays the firm's.** If misses are found, the firm describes the kind of miss (for example "nicknames in file notes"); any fix is a general rule, not one tuned to the firm's documents.
- **No legal advice.** The firm's risk contact reviews the research memo with the firm's own adviser.

### Roles

| Role | Who | Time needed |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsoring partner | A disputes partner at the firm | 30 minutes at the start and at the end |
| Pilot users | 2 to 5 lawyers or clerks | About 2 to 3 hours a week (an estimate) |
| IT contact | Firm IT or IT vendor | About 2 hours in week 1 |
| Risk contact | Risk or compliance partner | Time to agree the review with the firm's adviser; the memo asks the advocate to check every source against the official text |
| Lawgistics | [owner to name] | Onboarding, support, weekly check-in |

### Timeline

| When | What happens | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Before day 1 | Kick-off call. Agree users, success measures and the made-up test folder. IT reads the security statement. | Signed pilot letter |
| Week 1 | IT installs Cloak and runs the offline check (block it in the firewall, then use it). One-hour training on the sample matter. Each user times their current way of preparing a small folder for an AI tool, as a baseline. | IT check result; baseline times |
| Week 2 | Users cloak the firm's own made-up folder. They count every real name or identifier still visible before review, and after review. They send the cloaked copies to the firm's chosen AI tool, restore the drafts and note what the checks found. | Miss counts; review times; restore results |
| Week 3 | If the firm has a Cloak licence and agrees: limited use on real matters chosen by the sponsoring partner. If not: more made-up scenarios (Malay documents, scanned bundles, spreadsheets, emails). | Further measurements |
| Week 4 | Repeat the timing on a second made-up folder. Short user survey. Wrap-up meeting. | Pilot report (written from the firm's own figures) |
| Day 30 | Decision: buy, extend or stop. | Decision |

### Success measures (agree the targets with the firm at kick-off)

| Measure | How it is measured | Suggested target |
|---|---|---|
| Works offline | IT blocks Cloak in the firewall and runs a made-up matter | Pass |
| Set up | Pilot users with Cloak installed and a first matter created by the end of week 1 | All pilot users |
| Time to prepare a folder | Minutes with Cloak (including review) against the week 1 baseline, on comparable made-up folders | To be measured; the firm sets the bar it needs |
| Visible real details before review | Real names and identifiers still visible in cloaked text before review, per file, on the firm's made-up folder | Recorded and reported; no target |
| Visible real details after review | The same count in exported copies, after the review screen | Zero found in the firm's own read-through |
| Review effort | "Not sure" items per file and reviewer minutes per matter | Recorded |
| Restore | Share of AI drafts restored with no failed check; tag problems flagged | Recorded |
| Users would keep using it | Survey: "Would you use Cloak on your next suitable matter?" | At least 60% of pilot users say yes |
| Risk sign-off | Risk contact has reviewed the research memo with the firm's adviser | Done |

A pilot can "fail" usefully: if the firm finds misses that matter to it, the report says so and Lawgistics decides whether general rules can fix them.

## 5. Open questions for the owner

Questions that the sales pack cannot answer from the code, the documents or the research, and that a buyer, an IT manager or a risk partner is likely to ask. Each needs your decision or a fact only you have.

1. **Who is Lawgistics?** Company name and registration number, office address, and who answers support. A buyer asks for them, and the one-pager, the letter and the security statement still have placeholders for the contact (name, email, phone). The app's own support contact is also a placeholder.
2. **When can a firm use Cloak on real matters?** It depends on you making the vendor licence key, building Cloak with it and issuing licences. Give a target date, or say there is none.
3. **What will it cost after the pilot?** Every price above is a suggestion. Approve a price list before anyone quotes one. Decide the Option B band for 11 to 20 lawyers.
4. **Code signing.** Choose the Windows route (certificate type, or cloud signing) and enrol for Apple's developer programme; buy the certificates; try the first signed build, which has never run. Until then installers are unsigned.
5. **How firms get the installer, and whether you publish a SHA-256 checksum for each.** The pack makes no promise about either.
6. **Electron 33.** The window is built on Electron 33, which is old and has no auto-update. Decide whether to upgrade before sale, and how often you will ship security updates as new installers.
7. **Version numbers.** Builds made before 15 November 2026 and the current build are both called 0.1.0, and the earlier ones read some scanned PDF pages as blank. Give each build its own number, and tell any firm that received an earlier build.
8. **Firm-wide settings.** IT cannot force Strict mode, switch off "Remember the password on this computer" or raise the minimum password length. Decide whether to build any of these.
9. **Screens to change a matter password and replace a recovery code.** Not built. Give a target date. Until then, a lawyer who leaves can be shut out only by making a new matter.
10. **Restoring a draft after the date numbers change.** Cloak does not warn when a draft made before a new document changed the date numbers is restored. Decide whether to add a warning.
11. **Pilot terms.** No pilot terms or licence agreement exist. A Malaysian advocate should draft one page (licence, warranty, liability if a name gets through, confidentiality) before the letter is sent.
12. **Support hours and response time**, and who answers.
13. **The research memo.** It has no named author, and no advocate has reviewed it. Decide who writes the covering note and who the reviewing advocate is. After the Bar circulars and the Commissioner's texts have been read in full (see the legal check note), update the memo.
14. **The 22-file measurement.** It was run once, on 12 November 2026, on an earlier version, and counted by hand from the output. It is not recorded in the project's documents. Record it (date, version, method) in the names measurement note, or confirm the figure, before it is quoted again.
15. **The speed run.** The 300-page, 60-page and 5,000-row run is not finished. The pack quotes only the figures in the IT guide.
16. **Review time per matter** has not been measured. The pilot measures reviewer minutes per matter; agree a target with the first firm.
17. **Windows silent install.** The silent-install options in the IT guide are the installer tool's standard ones and have not been tried on a Windows computer.
